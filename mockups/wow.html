Sam Duckworth Photography — mobile / Instagram

Five things
worth building

All five are working prototypes on your real photos, not pictures of ideas — tap and drag them. They're built around one problem: someone taps your Instagram bio link, lands on a phone, and decides in about three seconds. They just left a fast, gestural, full-screen app. A normal website is a downgrade. So these do two jobs — feel native first, then offer the things Instagram physically can't.

01

Stories

The bridge from Instagram

A full-screen, tap-through viewer with progress bars at the top — the exact grammar someone arrives with. Tap right to advance, left to go back. Why it works: zero learning curve, and it inverts the usual problem. Instead of asking an Instagram visitor to learn a website, the website meets them where they already are, then quietly offers the two things the app can't: the full-resolution frame, and a way to buy it.

Try it — tap the right side of the screen

02

The edit

Highest share rate

Drag to wipe between the flat original and the finished frame. Why it works: before/after is consistently the most-shared thing a photographer posts, because it shows the craft rather than the result — it reframes you from "someone with a good camera" to someone with an eye. It also gives people a reason to come back to the site, which a static gallery never does. Note: the "before" here is your finished file pushed back to a flat state as a stand-in — only you have the real originals.

Try it — drag the handle left and right

03

On your wall

Sells prints

Pick a size and see the framed print against a room at true scale. Why it works: the single biggest reason people don't buy art online is that they can't picture the size — "is 40×50 big?" is unanswerable in a product grid. Showing it next to a sofa answers it instantly. This is the one idea here that directly moves money, and it slots into Framed Editions when the shop opens.

Try it — switch sizes and watch the frame scale

04

Picks

Works before the shop does

Heart anything; a tray slides up with what you've chosen and one button to send it to you. Why it works: it captures intent while the shop is still unbuilt — instead of "come back when it launches", someone leaves you a shortlist and an email address. It's also a reason to keep scrolling: collecting is a loop, browsing isn't.

Try it — tap a few hearts

05

Native transitions

Cheapest win here

Tap a tile and the photo expands into the detail view instead of the page blinking. Why it works: this is what separates "an app" from "a website" in a way nobody can articulate but everyone feels. The browser now does it natively — the View Transitions API shipped in Safari on iOS 18.1, so it works for essentially your whole Instagram audience, degrades to a plain cut on anything older, and costs almost nothing in code.

Try it — tap any tile
