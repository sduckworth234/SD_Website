Six ways to
show the places

Six treatments for the location cards, all running on your real locations, real photo counts and real capture years. The brief was minimal, professional-photography, not drone-themed — and above all, something that makes a first-time visitor swipe or scroll instead of leaving. Start in phone view, since that's where the bounce risk is.

—
01

Editorial rows

Fewest, biggest

One place per row, name and count set beside the frame rather than on top of it. Why it holds attention: a full-width row is unambiguously content, not a thumbnail grid to skim past — and because each row is large, the eye has to travel, which is what scroll depth actually is. On a phone the image stacks above the copy and every row is a full tap target.

02

Peek rail

Best on mobile

A snap carousel where the next card is deliberately cut off at the right edge. Why it holds attention: the partial card is the single strongest "there's more here" signal on a touch screen — people swipe it without being told to. The progress bar underneath shows how far in they are, which pulls them to the end. Costs one screen of vertical space instead of twenty-six.

03

Print stack

Most tactile

Each place is a small pile of prints; hovering or tapping fans them out. Why it holds attention: it says "there are more photos inside" with an object rather than a number, and it ties the home page to the Framed Editions side of the business. The fan is a 600ms flourish, not a page-blocking animation.

04

Split curtain

Most minimal

A typographic index where each row carries a small greyscale sliver that widens and turns colour on hover. Why it holds attention: it reads as a contents page, so a visitor scans the whole list rather than stopping at the first tile — and the reveal rewards curiosity. This is the most restrained option and the fastest to load.

05

Contact sheet

Most archival

A tight numbered grid that develops from greyscale on hover. Why it holds attention: density reads as depth of work — twenty-six places look like a real body of work rather than a thin new site. The trade-off is that individual photos get small, so it suits a secondary "all places" block more than the main event.

06

Chapter panels

Most immersive

Full-bleed bands, one per place, with the count set large in the corner. Why it holds attention: nothing competes inside a band, so each scroll lands on a single photograph — closest to the feeling of the lightbox, and the strongest showcase for the photography itself. Best paired with a short curated list rather than all twenty-six.
