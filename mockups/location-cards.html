Six ways to
show the places

Now showing the top 10 places by photo count (most recent year breaking ties), rather than all 26. Treatment 01 is the merge you asked for — editorial cards, image above type, in a rail that scrolls. 04 no longer depends on hover: it picks its live row from scroll position, so it works on a phone. Start in phone view.

—
01

Editorial rail

The merge

Editorial cards — photo on top, name and count underneath on the paper, nothing written over the image — laid out in a horizontal snap rail with the next card peeking. Why it holds attention: the peek is the strongest "swipe me" cue on touch, while the type sitting below the frame keeps it feeling like a printed portfolio rather than a row of banners. Ten places cost one screen of height instead of ten. The rail ends on an "All 26 places" card so it has a destination rather than just running out.

02

Editorial rows

Desktop-strong

The full-width version: one place per row, image and copy side by side, alternating. Why it holds attention: unambiguously content rather than a thumbnail grid, and each row is a large tap target. Stronger on desktop than 01; on a phone it stacks and gets tall, which is the trade-off the rail solves.

03

Print stack

Most tactile

Each place is a small pile of prints that fans open. Why it holds attention: it says "more photos inside" with an object rather than a number, and ties the home page to Framed Editions. On touch the fan is driven by scroll rather than hover, same as 04.

04

Split curtain

Now scroll-driven

You were right that this was broken on mobile — hover doesn't exist on touch, so the list would have stayed grey forever. The live row is now chosen by scroll position: whichever row is crossing the middle of the screen expands its sliver, comes to full colour and shifts its name. Scrolling hands it to the next one. Why it holds attention: the page appears to respond to you, so scrolling becomes the interaction — and it still reads as a quiet contents page. Desktop keeps hover too, so a mouse feels direct rather than laggy.

05

Contact sheet

Most archival

A tight numbered grid that develops from greyscale — also scroll-driven on touch. Why it holds attention: density reads as depth of work, which matters while the site is new. Best as the "all places" block underneath a curated set rather than the main event.

06

Chapter panels

Most immersive

Full-bleed bands, one per place, count set large. Why it holds attention: nothing competes inside a band, so each scroll lands on a single photograph. The strongest showcase for the work, and the most vertical height per place.
