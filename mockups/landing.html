Sam Duckworth Photography — the landing screen

Five openings

The first two seconds, on a phone, for someone who just tapped your Instagram bio. All five run on your real photos — hit Replay on any of them to watch the load again. The current hero is a full-bleed photo with the name over it; worth knowing that pattern now appears in under 12% of Awwwards winners, down from being the default. These are five ways out of it.

01

Ignition

Shows the body of work instantly

Opens as a contact sheet of your archive, which collapses inward into a single frame. Why it hooks: in under a second it says "there is a lot here" and then "and this is the standard" — breadth and quality in one gesture. It also answers the question a first-time visitor actually has, which isn't "who is this" but "is there enough here to be worth my time".

~950ms, then settled
02

The deal

Ties to Framed Editions

Prints deal onto the surface and settle into a loose stack. Why it hooks: it's tactile and it makes the photographs feel like objects rather than files — which is exactly the story the print shop needs to tell. The warm paper background is also a deliberate break from every other photographer's black hero.

~1.3s, staggered
03

Knockout

Most editorial

Your name set enormous, with the photograph showing through the letterforms. Why it hooks: it's the most designed of the five and the most memorable — it reads as a brand rather than a template. The trade-off is honest: the photo is decoration here, not the subject, so it works best if you pick one very graphic frame and change it rarely.

~900ms · CSS background-clip, no images stacked
04

Filmstrip

Motion without video

Two columns of frames drifting in opposite directions behind the wordmark, forever. Why it hooks: movement holds attention where a static hero doesn't, and it never stops — so there's no "the animation finished, now what". It costs a fraction of a video: no autoplay policy fights, no multi-megabyte download, and it's two transforms.

Continuous · 2 animated transforms total
05

Golden hour

Feels alive, costs nothing

The hero matches the time of day where the visitor is standing — dawn at dawn, last light in the evening. Why it hooks: it's a small personal shock; people notice a site that seems to know something about them. It's also the cheapest idea here — one line of JS picking from four curated photos.

Drag the slider to move through the day. This demo picks the four frames by hand; wiring it for real would need a time-of-day tag on the photo, since capture time isn't in the public data.

Slider = the visitor's local time

If I had to pick

  1. 01 Ignition — it answers the real first-visit question ("is there enough here?") faster than anything else, and it reuses the archive you already have.
  2. 05 Golden hour — least effort by a distance, and the only one that makes the site feel like it's running rather than published. Pairs with any of the others.
  3. 04 Filmstrip — the safest upgrade on what's there now: same full-bleed feel, but moving, and far cheaper than the video route most photographers take.

Two things worth saying

  • All five are transform and opacity only — no layout animation, nothing that repaints per frame. They cost roughly what the current hero costs.
  • A load animation plays once. Anything longer than about a second of it is spending your visitor's attention before they've seen a photograph — which is why every one of these settles inside ~1.3s.
